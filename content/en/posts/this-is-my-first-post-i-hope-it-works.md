I have decided that I want to start writing blog posts. Not for any particular reason other than I feel like it's good for my brain. It's an enjoyable creative experience. It helps me keep track of thoughts I want to hold onto, that otherwise get lost into the ether. 

I haven't decided what type of things I will post here. Maybe book reviews, ideas, recipes I vouch for. 

I want my own website so I can speak in a more authentic way that doesn't conform to a social media platform's social conventions. It's a journal, I suppose. Something I can reference in the future.

Life lately has been incredibly rewarding. I have moved into my own apartment in Birmingham, living a simple, but fruitful life. I go to the gym almost everyday, I buy fresh fruit and veg from the local market, I cook, I bake, I read, and I dabble in some live music. About to start my placement, working for the University, volunteering. I have a good sense of momentum, the regularity proving to be more reassuring than dull. 

Moving into a new place has been a big moment for me. I feel a strong sense of agency and connection to Birmingham in a way I wasn't quite able to achieve as a commuter student. I am cooking an incredible amount of Crumble at the moment, which is my way of justifying bulk buying loads of fruit from the markets. 

There is something quite wonderful about how little my needs are. Weightlifting is a simple way to organise my mind and be kind to my body. Getting stronger over time is so rewarding, and I think it reflects well on what a good life, for me at least, looks like,. I don't need any Einstein Eureka moments to get to where I need to be.  I just need to reap the cumulative benefits of putting in the hours - doing the obvious thing each day, over time.

Cooking is another one of those activities I find greatly empowering. To be able to understand the personalities of different flavour combinations, cook with my intuition, connect to my senses, and in an ideal world, share the fruits of my labour with loved ones, is one of life's many blessings. I have my parents to thank for raising us with such good quality ingredients, soul food. 

Reading, is probably the main habit I need to restore momentum for. I was on a roll in the summer, reading for at least an hour a day, which for my standards, is pretty good going (Its been my new years resolution since 2020 to read more). I may post some book reviews on here to hold myself accountable. We'll see.

Writing this on my lunchbreak. I'm representing the UoB Careers network this week. Its been really good to chat to lots of new faces. 

Finally, I recognise that the format of this post, is reflective of my disorganised brain, but whilst its fresh, I wanted to note that this blog is hopefully going to help me develop my own voice in my writing style. I am content with being imperfect here, I want to reclaim my intellectual agency without AI. Not enough Students, in my opinion recognise the importance of being able to think, reason, and question for themselves. A lot of people are outsourcing processing to LLMs that is essential for a dignified human experience. Its a blessing to engage with the world through a normal human lens.
